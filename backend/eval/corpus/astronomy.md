# The Solar System

## The Sun

The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star at the center of the Solar System. It contains
about 99.86 percent of the Solar System's total mass. The Sun's core reaches a
temperature of approximately 15 million degrees Celsius, hot enough to sustain the
nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium. Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes and
20 seconds to reach Earth.

## Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet in the Solar System and the closest to the Sun. It
completes one orbit around the Sun in just 88 Earth days, the shortest year of any
planet. Mercury has no atmosphere to retain heat, so its surface temperature swings
from 430 degrees Celsius during the day to minus 180 degrees Celsius at night.

## Venus

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the hottest planet in the Solar System,
with a surface temperature of about 465 degrees Celsius, caused by a runaway
greenhouse effect from its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere. Venus rotates so slowly
that a single day on Venus is longer than its year: one rotation takes 243 Earth days.

## Earth

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet to support life. It
is the largest of the four rocky inner planets. Earth's atmosphere is about 78 percent
nitrogen and 21 percent oxygen. Earth has one natural satellite, the Moon, which is
about 384,400 kilometers away on average.

## Mars

Mars, often called the Red Planet because of iron oxide on its surface, is the fourth
planet from the Sun. Mars hosts Olympus Mons, the tallest known volcano and second
tallest known mountain in the Solar System, standing about 22 kilometers high. Mars
has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos.

## Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System, with a mass more than twice that of
all the other planets combined. Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a giant storm that has
been raging for at least 350 years and is large enough to fit two or three Earths
inside it. Jupiter has at least 95 known moons, the largest of which is Ganymede.

## Saturn

Saturn is the second largest planet and is famous for its extensive ring system, made
mostly of ice particles with a smaller amount of rocky debris and dust. Saturn's moon
Titan is the only moon in the Solar System known to have a dense atmosphere and
stable liquid lakes, made of methane and ethane, on its surface.

## Uranus and Neptune

Uranus is unique among the planets in that it rotates on its side, with an axial tilt
of about 98 degrees. Neptune, the outermost known planet, has the strongest sustained
winds of any planet, reaching speeds of up to 2,100 kilometers per hour. Neptune was
the first planet located through mathematical prediction rather than direct
observation.
