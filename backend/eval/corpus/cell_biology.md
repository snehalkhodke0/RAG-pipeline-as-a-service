# Fundamentals of Cell Biology

## The Cell Membrane

The cell membrane, also called the plasma membrane, is a phospholipid bilayer that
separates the interior of a cell from its external environment. It is selectively
permeable, controlling which substances can enter or exit the cell through channels
and transport proteins embedded in the membrane.

## The Nucleus

The nucleus is a membrane-bound organelle that contains most of a eukaryotic cell's
genetic material, organized as DNA in chromosomes. The nucleolus, found inside the
nucleus, is responsible for producing ribosomes. The nuclear envelope has pores that
regulate the movement of molecules between the nucleus and the cytoplasm.

## Mitochondria

Mitochondria are often referred to as the powerhouse of the cell because they generate
most of the cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate, or ATP, used as a source of
chemical energy. Mitochondria have their own DNA, separate from the nuclear DNA, and
are believed to have originated from ancient free-living bacteria through a process
called endosymbiosis.

## Ribosomes

Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis within a cell. They can be found floating
freely in the cytoplasm or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum. Ribosomes translate
messenger RNA sequences into chains of amino acids that fold into functional proteins.

## The Endoplasmic Reticulum and Golgi Apparatus

The endoplasmic reticulum comes in two forms: rough, which is studded with ribosomes
and involved in protein synthesis, and smooth, which is involved in lipid synthesis
and detoxification. The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and
lipids received from the endoplasmic reticulum before they are sent to their final
destination.

## Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts are organelles found in plant cells and algae that conduct photosynthesis,
converting light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. Like mitochondria,
chloroplasts contain their own DNA and are thought to have originated from
photosynthetic bacteria that were engulfed by an ancestral eukaryotic cell.

## The Cytoskeleton

The cytoskeleton is a network of protein filaments that gives a cell its shape,
enables cell movement, and facilitates the transport of materials within the cell. It
is made up of three main components: microfilaments, intermediate filaments, and
microtubules.
